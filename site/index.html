The free download manager that works like IDM, on Windows, macOS and Linux

UDM (Unified Download Manager) fetches each file over up to 16 connections, resumes after interruptions, catches downloads from your browser and saves streamed videos as one file. Open source, with no ads, no trial and no sign-up.

Nothing new to learn

The browser hands its downloads over, a save-as dialog asks where they go, and the categories, queues, scheduler and download windows are where IDM users expect them.

Catch downloads from the browser

The extension hands downloads from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Opera, Firefox and Safari to UDM with their cookies and referrer, so files behind a sign-in work. Download File Info then asks for the name, category and folder.

Set up browser capture

See every connection

Each download has a window of its own with its speed, the time left and the parts coming in. If the computer crashes or the connection drops, it picks up where it stopped. New link gives an expired download a fresh address and carries on.

Resume a broken download

Save the video on the page

A button over a playing video lists each quality, audio track and subtitle. UDM fetches HLS, DASH and HDS streams and joins them into one file with ffmpeg, and reads YouTube, Vimeo, Facebook and Instagram pages with yt-dlp.

Download a video from a page

Download overnight

Queues start on a schedule, can wake the computer, and put it to sleep or shut it down when they finish. A quota and speed limits keep the connection free for everyone else.

Schedule a queue

Torrents in the same list

Paste a magnet link or open a .torrent file. Pick the files you want, and the torrent waits in queues, follows the speed limit and seeds for a while when it is done, like any other download.

And the rest of the toolbox

New to download managers? Start with your first download.

  • Faster downloads

    Up to 16 connections per file, or streams over one connection when the server speaks HTTP/2. FTP works too.

  • Many files at once

    Numbered patterns like photo[001-120].jpg, pasted lists, every link on a page, or a site grabber that crawls a site for the files you want.

  • Keeps going

    A background program owns the downloads, so they continue after you close the browser and the window.

  • Scripts and AI assistants

    IDM's command-line switches, a udm-cli tool, a versioned automation API, and an MCP server for Claude, Cursor and VS Code.

  • Proxies and limits

    System, PAC, HTTP, HTTPS and SOCKS proxies with site logins, a speed limit for all downloads or one, and a download quota.

  • Your language

    21 languages, with Hebrew, Arabic and Persian laid out right to left, and Light, Dark and High contrast themes.

Questions

Is UDM free?

Yes. UDM is free and open source under the MIT License. There is no trial, no serial number, no account and no ads.

Is there an IDM alternative for Mac and Linux?

UDM works the way Internet Download Manager does, with the same save-as dialog, categories, queues, scheduler and download windows, and it runs on Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux. The macOS build is experimental.

Which browsers does UDM work with?

UDM's extension catches downloads from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Opera, Firefox and Safari, with their cookies and referrer, so files behind a sign-in work too.

Can UDM download videos?

Yes. It downloads HLS, DASH and HDS streams in the quality you pick and joins them into one file with ffmpeg, and it reads YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and Vimeo pages with yt-dlp.

Does a download resume after a crash or restart?

Yes. UDM resumes after a crash, a restart or a dropped connection without starting over, and its downloads keep going after the browser is closed.
